# AgriLoss decision-support evaluation protocol

**Status:** Ready to run. No participant sessions have been conducted and there are no measured impact results.

## Evaluation question

When planners or cooperative staff investigate a crop issue, does using AgriLoss help them produce a more evidence-based, locally verifiable next step than their current way of reviewing the same survey information?

This formative study tests decision quality and workflow usefulness on realistic planning scenarios. It does not measure changes in farmer losses, income, productivity, or welfare.

## Participants and setting

Recruit 8–12 intended users for a first formative round, such as district or national agricultural planners, cooperative operations staff, extension staff, or analysts who prepare crop follow-up plans. Aim for more than one role and organization. This small purposive sample can reveal usability and reasoning problems; it cannot represent all Rwandan users.

Run individual 45–60 minute sessions with a facilitator. Obtain informed agreement to participate and take notes. Explain that the exercise evaluates the tool, not the participant. Use fictional planning locations and no personal or farmer-identifying information. Keep coded study records in a private working location and do not commit completed records to the repository.

## Comparison design

Use two matched crop-investigation scenarios. Match them as closely as possible on task type, evidence volume, and difficulty; for example, two storage/collection cases or two processing/value-addition cases. Prepare each case with the [scenario-card template](impact-evaluation-scenario-template.md).

Each participant completes one scenario with their usual/source-based workflow and the other with AgriLoss. Give both conditions the same underlying crop evidence and the same local planning facts. Counterbalance which scenario is used with AgriLoss and which condition comes first across participants. Record any differences in materials or assistance. Do not let the facilitator explain the answer.

Use these four sequences in rotation so scenario and order effects are spread across conditions:

| Sequence | First task | Second task |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scenario A, source-based | Scenario B, AgriLoss |
| 2 | Scenario B, AgriLoss | Scenario A, source-based |
| 3 | Scenario A, AgriLoss | Scenario B, source-based |
| 4 | Scenario B, source-based | Scenario A, AgriLoss |

For each scenario, ask the participant to:

1. State the next decision or follow-up they would recommend to a planner or cooperative.
2. Point to the sample evidence supporting it, including the crop, measure, count, and valid denominator where available.
3. Identify the location-specific conditions they would verify and who they would involve.
4. List the key missing information or checks required before an operational or investment decision.
5. State what the survey evidence does not establish and rate confidence from 0 to 100.

In the AgriLoss condition, ask the participant to use the crop workspace, enter the scenario's planning location and conditions, submit for recommendation review, and preview the report. In the source-based condition, allow the participant to use the team's current source or reporting workflow. Record which tools and reference materials they use.

## Decision-quality rubric

Score each dimension from 0 to 2 after the session. Score the recorded answer without regard to which condition produced it.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence use | No relevant evidence or a material misread | Relevant measure named, but denominator, count, or meaning is incomplete | Correct crop-level measure and supporting count/valid denominator are identified and interpreted accurately |
| Scope and uncertainty | Makes an unsupported national, crop-by-location, causal, or current-market claim | Mentions a limitation but still overstates what the evidence says | Separates sample evidence from local input and states a relevant limitation correctly |
| Follow-up action | No clear next step | General check with no method or actor | Specific, verifiable field check with an appropriate actor and evidence to collect |
| Decision gate | Proposes a process, facility, or investment without key validation | Names some missing inputs | Identifies the important local supply, buyer, price, quality, yield, cost, or facility checks before a decision |
| Local plan | No meaningful location or condition context | Location/condition is recorded but does not shape the plan | Location and stated conditions lead to a focused next step and a useful handoff/report |

Total decision-quality score: **0–10 per scenario**. Also record these critical interpretation errors separately: treating a positive-response share as a physical loss rate; joining the province summary to the selected crop; treating 2024 prices as current; describing the sample as a national estimate; or treating a process example as proof of demand or profitability.

If possible, have two reviewers score anonymized session notes independently. Reconcile score differences after recording both initial scores, and refine rubric wording before the next round if reviewers disagree repeatedly.

## Measures and analysis

**Primary measure:** Difference in decision-quality scores between the AgriLoss and source-based conditions under the counterbalanced scenario assignments. Report every participant/scenario/condition score, the median and range of participant-level differences, how many scored higher/the same/lower with AgriLoss, and results by scenario and sequence. The small purposive sample and different matched scenarios cannot isolate the tool effect conclusively, so keep all findings descriptive.

**Secondary measures:** task completion, time per scenario, report usefulness (1–5), confidence (0–100), critical interpretation errors, and participant-reported changes they would make to a real planning workflow. Summarize errors by type and count. Preserve short, non-identifying quotes only with participant agreement.

Before recruiting, review the scenarios, rubric, and minimum useful improvement with intended users. Record the agreed improvement threshold and acceptable critical-error threshold here: **[complete before sessions]**. A provisional prototype gate to discuss with participants is a median improvement of at least 1 point out of 10, with no increase in critical interpretation errors. This is a usability/decision-support gate, not proof of real-world impact.

If the dashboard helps users identify a better follow-up question, check whether that question is actually used in a planning meeting or analysis within 30–60 days. Record whether it was accepted, revised, or not used and why. That is evidence of workflow uptake. Any claim that the tool changed farmer or market outcomes requires a later, separately designed evaluation with a valid comparison and appropriate outcome measures.

## Session recording sheet

Use the [blank results template](impact-evaluation-results-template.csv), with one row for each participant/scenario/condition. Use `usual_source` or `agriloss` in the `condition` column, `1` or `2` in `order`, and `0`/`1` for each error flag (`1` means observed; leave blank if not scored). Fill report usefulness for the AgriLoss task only. Do not put participant names, contact details, farmer details, or identifiable free text in it. Keep any consent records separately from evaluation scores.

## Reporting rule

Publish only the number and role mix of participants, study dates, scenario and comparison method, score results, critical errors, limitations, and changes made to the tool. State clearly that formative task performance is not evidence of reduced crop losses or improved livelihoods. Update the status only after sessions have been conducted and the results are documented.
