# Evidence-backed follow-up recommendations

The Crop workspace converts reported SAS 2024 patterns into questions for planners, cooperatives, processors, and buyers to verify. It identifies where to investigate and which process routes might merit comparison. It does not predict returns, establish demand, or recommend equipment or capital spending.

## Planner workflow

1. Choose the storage/collection or processing/value-addition track, then select a crop from the sample table.
2. Enter a district, cooperative, market, or collection area and, if useful, a planning period. Optionally select a province to view its separate SAS summary.
3. Select the local constraints that partners want to investigate and add short notes without personal information.
4. Submit the context to review the crop-level recommendation, its survey evidence, and field actions prompted by the selected conditions.
5. Preview and download the Markdown report. It records the crop signal, location and period entered, any separate province context, selected conditions, local actions, survey data-quality checks, and a decision gate.

The location, period, notes, and conditions are user-entered planning context. They do not filter SAS records or make the crop and province summaries a joined result. The report labels survey evidence and local inputs in separate sections.

## Storage and collection investigation

The storage track flags a crop when its positive storage-stage loss share (`s2q47`) or transport-stage loss share (`s2q46`) is positive and at or above the 75th percentile among named crop groups with publishable summaries. Ties are included. Each flag shows the positive-response count, valid-answer denominator, and comparison cutoff. The queue is a relative screen across unweighted, pooled 2024 seasons; the percentile is not a safe-loss threshold, a national estimate, or a ranking of investment need.

For a storage-stage flag, the brief directs follow-up on drying, time in storage, pests, facility condition, and questionnaire skip patterns. For a transport-stage flag, it directs local mapping of collection handoffs, routes, handling damage, time, volume, and cost. SAS production summaries do not provide enough local operating detail to identify the cause or choose an intervention.

## Processing options to evaluate

The processing track considers reported transformation use separately for food (`s2q25_1`), drink (`s2q25_2`), and other uses (`s2q25_3`). It displays a crop/use option only when that category has at least 100 positive records and at least 100 valid answers. The 100-record rule is a display and disclosure rule; it is not a viability threshold. Evidence includes the positive count, share of valid answers, median positive quantity, sale share, and, where a crop/channel group has enough positive-price records, a historical Season A 2024 median reported price. Missing market evidence is shown when no crop/channel price group clears the display rule.

Examples in the workspace are possible routes grounded in documented Rwandan research or processor examples:

- Maize food use: compare local or contract milling into flour. [RAB documented a maize-flour company in Gisagara](https://www.rab.gov.rw/1-1/news-details/the-director-general-visits-agro-processing-industries-in-gisagara-district).
- Cassava food use: evaluate flour products with an existing processor. A [RAB annual report documents cassava flour and porridge research](https://www.rab.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/RAB/Publications/Reports/RAB-Annual_report___2012_2013.pdf).
- Cassava other use: first clarify what respondents meant by “other”; if cassava peels are available, assess a safe peel-to-feed route. [RAB and IITA demonstrated cassava-peel feed processing](https://www.rab.gov.rw/1-1/news-details/rab-iita-train-farmers-on-processing-cassava-peels-into-animal-feed).
- Bush and climbing bean food use: compare flour or blended products with existing processors. A [RAB annual report describes beans processed or blended into flours](https://www.rab.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/RAB/Publications/Reports/RAB_Annual_2018-2019_FY.pdf).
- Banana drink use: evaluate juice or fermented-beverage processing. [RAB documented banana juice and beer processors in Gisagara](https://www.rab.gov.rw/1-1/news-details/the-director-general-visits-agro-processing-industries-in-gisagara-district).

These references establish that a route has been documented or evaluated in Rwanda. They do not show that the route is active in the selected crop group or location, meets food-safety requirements, has a buyer, or is profitable today. Other crop/use groups receive a generic instruction to define a product with an existing processor before comparing processes.

## Evidence required before a decision

Before selecting a process or proposing investment, verify local seasonal supply and collection volumes; product quality and safety requirements; processor and buyer interest; current prices and sales terms; conversion yields; energy, labor, packaging, transport, and maintenance costs; and the alternatives of contract processing, existing facilities, and no intervention. For storage or transport, also check facility condition, drying and handling practices, routes, travel time, and measured losses. A pilot should evaluate outcomes separately.

The public dashboard uses grouped, unweighted sample summaries and is marked provisional pending NISR disclosure review. Crop and province summaries are separate margins and must not be read as a crop-by-province result. Historical SAS prices are not a live market feed. The underlying survey does not establish causes, processor demand, costs, or future returns.
